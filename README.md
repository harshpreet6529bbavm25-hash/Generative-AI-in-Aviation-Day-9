# 1. Platform-Specific Marketing Copy

The key principle is that the format and tone should change according to the platform, but the underlying campaign facts must remain consistent.

For AeroVista Student Saver:

- LinkedIn: professional, informative and credible.
- Instagram: shorter, engaging and student-friendly.
- Advertisement headline: very short and attention-grabbing.
- Promotional email: structured, explanatory and action-oriented.
- Website: detailed, accurate and easy to navigate.

The most important accuracy rule is "up to 10%". The campaign does not guarantee a 10% discount to every student.

|---|---|

| Availability | Seats are limited and subject to availability |

## Claims That Must NOT Be Invented

The campaign brief does not support claims that:

- Every student receives 10%.
- The offer applies to every route.
- Baggage is free.
- Tickets are refundable.
- There are no additional charges.
- AeroVista Student Saver is the cheapest fare.
- Seats are guaranteed.

Changing "up to 10%" to "10% off" would materially change the offer.

# 3. Master Marketing Prompt

## Prompt Components

**Role:**  
Marketing communications executive for the fictional airline AeroVista.

**Task:**  
Create platform-specific promotional content for AeroVista Student Saver.

**Context:**  
AeroVista is launching a student-fare campaign on selected domestic routes.

**Target audience:**  
College and university students.

**Constraints:**  
Use only verified campaign facts. Do not invent benefits, prices, routes, baggage benefits, refundability or guaranteed availability.

**Output formats:**

- LinkedIn post
- Instagram copy
- Advertisement headline
- Promotional email
- Website copy

**Tone:**  
Platform-appropriate, clear, credible, engaging and accurate.

## Master Prompt

> Act as a marketing communications executive for the fictional airline AeroVista. Using only the verified campaign brief provided, create platform-specific promotional content for the AeroVista Student Saver campaign.
>
> The target audience is college and university students. The campaign applies to selected domestic routes and offers up to 10% off the base fare. The booking period is 1–15 October and the travel period is 15 October–15 December. Valid student identification is required. Baggage, taxes, cancellation and change conditions are subject to applicable fare rules. Seats are limited and subject to availability. Customers can book through the official AeroVista website and mobile application.
>
> Do not claim that every student receives 10%, that the offer applies to all routes, that baggage is free, that tickets are refundable, that there are no additional charges, that the offer is the cheapest available fare or that seats are guaranteed. Do not invent additional benefits, prices or policy conditions.
>
> Adapt the format and tone to each platform while keeping all underlying campaign facts consistent.

## Discussion

The tone and length should change because each platform serves a different communication purpose and audience behaviour.

LinkedIn generally allows more professional context, while Instagram requires concise and visually engaging copy. An advertisement headline must communicate the core message almost immediately, whereas an email can provide more detailed conditions and a clear call to action.

However, changing the presentation must not change the underlying facts.

For example, "up to 10%" must remain "up to 10%" across platforms. It should not become "10% guaranteed" simply because stronger wording may appear more attractive.

# 9. Identify Non-Compliant Marketing Copy

The supplied caption contains several claims that are not supported by the campaign brief.

| Claim | Supported? | Why Is It Risky? | Required Correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Every student receives 10% | No | Brief says up to 10%, not guaranteed 10% for every student. | Change to "up to 10% off the base fare." |
| Offer applies to every domestic flight | No | Brief specifies selected domestic routes. | Change to "selected domestic routes." |
| Free baggage | No | Baggage is subject to applicable fare rules. | Remove the claim. |
| Completely refundable tickets | No | Refundability is not stated and depends on fare rules. | Remove the claim. |
| No additional charges | No | Taxes and applicable fare rules are mentioned. | Remove the claim. |
| Unlimited seats | No | Brief says seats are limited and subject to availability. | State "Seats are limited and subject to availability." |
| Cheapest student flights in India | No | No price comparison or cheapest-fare claim is provided. | Remove the claim entirely. |

## Main Problem

The original caption changes several qualified facts into absolute promises.

Examples:

- "Up to 10%" → "10% guaranteed"
- "Selected domestic routes" → "Every domestic flight"

These changes materially alter the campaign information.

# 11. Fact-Difference Check

# 12. Register Check

Register refers to the style and level of language appropriate for a particular audience and communication situation.

| Phrase | Classification | Suggested Rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| Grab this insane deal now! | Casual / Exaggerated | Explore the AeroVista Student Saver offer today. |
| Book through the official AeroVista channels. | Professional | Book through the official AeroVista website or mobile application. |
| Everyone gets the full discount! | Misleading | Eligible students may receive up to 10% off the base fare. |
| Terms and availability conditions apply. | Professional | Applicable fare rules and availability conditions apply. |
| The cheapest flight offer ever! | Exaggerated / Misleading | Explore the AeroVista Student Saver offer on selected domestic routes. |
| Students may receive up to 10% off the base fare. | Professional | No correction required. |

## Register Check Finding

The safest professional language uses specific, qualified statements rather than emotional or absolute claims.

For example, "up to 10% off" accurately reflects the campaign brief, whereas "everyone gets the full discount" changes a qualified offer into a guarantee.

# 16. Student Reflection

## 1. Why should one marketing brief be adapted for different platforms?

A single marketing brief should be adapted because each platform has a different audience, communication style and content format.

LinkedIn generally requires professional and informative communication, while Instagram can use shorter and more engaging language. An advertisement headline needs to communicate its message very quickly, whereas an email can provide more detailed information.

## 2. What is the difference between a fact and a promotional claim?

A fact is information that can be supported by the campaign brief, such as a date, eligibility condition or verified discount.

A promotional claim is language used to persuade the audience. Promotional language must still remain within the limits of the verified facts.

## 3. Why is "up to 10%" different from "10% guaranteed"?

"Up to 10%" establishes a maximum possible discount. It does not mean that every eligible customer receives exactly 10%.

"10% guaranteed" changes the meaning by promising the maximum discount to everyone who qualifies.

## 4. What is a fact-difference check?

A fact-difference check compares the original business or campaign brief with the AI-generated marketing content.

It identifies whether facts are the same, missing or changed.

## 5. What is a register check?

A register check examines whether the language is appropriate for the intended audience, platform and communication situation.

It helps identify language that is too casual, exaggerated, professional or potentially misleading.

## 6. Why must important offer conditions be included?

Important conditions protect the accuracy of the marketing message and help customers understand the actual offer.

Conditions such as selected routes, eligibility and availability can materially affect whether a customer can use an offer.

## 7. Give two examples of misleading marketing language.

Two examples are:

- "Everyone gets the full 10% discount."
- "Seats are unlimited."

Both statements go beyond what the campaign brief verifies.

## 8. How did the compliance checklist improve your content?

The checklist provided a final control before publication.

It helped verify dates, figures, conditions, claims, tone, brand consistency and the accuracy of the call to action.

## 9. How did peer review improve your campaign?

Peer review provided another perspective on whether the content was clear and whether any condition or unsupported statement could be misunderstood.

It can help identify problems that the original writer overlooks.

## 10. Why must AI-generated marketing content receive human approval before publication?

AI can produce fluent and persuasive marketing language while still introducing unsupported claims or changing the meaning of the original brief.

Human approval provides a final check of factual accuracy, conditions, brand requirements and compliance before the content reaches customers.

# 18. Key Learning

The most important lesson from Day 9 is:

> AI can adapt marketing language, but it must not adapt the facts.

For example:

**Verified:**
"Students may receive up to 10% off the base fare."

**Not acceptable:**
"Every student gets 10% off."

The first statement preserves the campaign's qualification, while the second creates a guarantee that the source does not support.

This is why the lab combines:

- Platform adaptation
- Fact-difference checking
- Register checking
- Compliance review
- Peer review
- Human approval

rather than evaluating marketing copy only on how attractive it sounds.
